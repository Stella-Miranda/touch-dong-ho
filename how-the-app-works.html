How the app works

Point at the painting. It tells you what you found.

An Android phone sits on a small desk stand, looking down at the relief. There is no glove, no sensor under the board, no markers printed on the painting — just the camera, the hand, and a voice. The whole thing is one loop, repeated for as long as someone keeps touching the picture.

↻

The loop

Six steps, then back to the beginning

1

Work out which painting is on the table

The app takes one frame and asks a vision model which of the three Đông Hồ pieces it is looking at — Lý ngư vọng nguyệt, Gà dạ xướng or Chăn trâu thổi sáo. The model answers with a short code, so the reply can be matched with a plain text check and nothing depends on it phrasing the title correctly.

2

Read that painting's story aloud

The introduction is not generated. Each of the three is a fixed script, written by us and spoken word for word, because that is where the cultural detail lives: the five virtues of the rooster, the cá chép hóa rồng legend behind the carp watching the moon, the lotus leaf over the boy on the buffalo. A model looking at a photograph would thin all of that out or get it wrong. Its job here is recognition only.

3

Find the hand

Every camera frame arrives already split into brightness and colour. The app samples the colour values on a grid and sorts them into two groups — the painting, and whatever is in front of it. The smaller, distinctly coloured group is the hand, and its centre is where the finger is.

This matters more than it sounds. The obvious approach is to look for a fixed range of skin tones, and the obvious approach fails for exactly the people you most want it to work for: a different skin tone, a warm lamp, a dim room. Splitting whatever is actually in the frame into two colours has no opinion about what a hand should look like.

4

Decide that a pause is a question

Hands wander. The app only treats a position as a question when the fingertip stays inside a 30 px radius for 800 ms. Shorter than that and every sweep across the board would set off an answer; longer and the thing feels unresponsive. The numbers came from watching people use it at a table, not from a spec.

5

Capture, ask, and start talking

That moment is turned into a small JPEG — downsampled as it is converted, on a background thread, so the camera preview never freezes — and sent to the model together with the point the finger is resting on.

The answer is streamed back and spoken sentence by sentence as it arrives, instead of waiting for the whole paragraph. Standing at the table, that is the difference between a device that is thinking and a device that is broken.

6

Let go and wait for the next one

Once the answer has been spoken, the app will not repeat itself for the same spot. The hand has to travel more than 100 px away before a new question can be asked — so resting a finger where it already is stays silent, and moving on starts the loop again at step 3.

※

Two decisions worth explaining

Running it on a free API quota

A booth session is a few hundred requests in an afternoon, and this project runs on free tiers. So the app does not use one model — it rotates between two fast ones, starting with a different one each capture, which splits a session's requests across two separate quota pools instead of draining one.

When both are rate-limited it falls back to a larger model with much higher limits but a much slower answer, and the interface says so rather than leaving someone standing in silence. These endpoints also fail transiently often enough that one attempt each isn't enough, so a capture works through the whole list up to three times with a short pause between passes. A healthy model answers on the first try, so in the normal case the retries cost nothing.

Mô hình được chọn theo hạn mức miễn phí của API, chứ không chỉ theo tốc độ hay chất lượng câu trả lời.

Built with

Flutter, running on Android. The camera stream, the hand-finding and the dwell timer all run on the phone; only the two questions — which painting is this, and what is under this finger — go out over the network. Text-to-speech is the phone's own Vietnamese voice.
